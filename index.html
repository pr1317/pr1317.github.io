Flagship

opslab

Operations analytics for BFSI back-office processes

A dependency-free Python toolkit for the questions that come up in life-and-pensions operations and that general-purpose data science libraries answer badly — the same NEST Pensions back-office work I lead on at TCS, taken further. Pure standard library, so it installs on a locked-down corporate laptop.

  • Process mining

    Discovers the directly-follows graph from the event log — rework loops, variants, bottlenecks ranked by total time rather than mean. Conformance is declarative, so segregation-of-duties breaches map onto a control an auditor recognises.

  • Statistical process control

    Individuals, moving-range, X-bar R, p, c and u charts with all eight Nelson run rules. Cp/Cpk separated from Pp/Ppk, one-sided specs reported honestly rather than mirrored into a flattering number.

  • SLA survival analysis

    Kaplan-Meier, Nelson-Aalen, log-rank and Cox proportional hazards with right-censoring. On the demo data the naive median is 46.7 working hours and the censoring-aware one is 52.8 — that gap is the bias.

  • Power BI model linter

    Twenty rules over TMSL, TMDL and PBIP models, run on a real DAX tokenizer rather than regexes. Exits non-zero, so it drops straight into CI.

Handwritten digit recognition

How far classical ML gets on MNIST before a CNN is needed

98.48% on the standard 10,000-image test set — 152 digits wrong — from a 784-256-10 network on deskewed pixels, trained on all 60,000 images in under a minute on two CPU cores.

The finding

Preprocessing beat model choice. Removing handwriting slant with an affine shear — the slope is cov(x,y)/var(y) in closed form — is worth +3.34 points on the same logistic regression. The gap between the worst and best model in the whole comparison is about four.

  • 98.48%test accuracy
  • 51 straining time
  • 152errors, mostly genuinely ambiguous

Customer churn analytics

Predicting churn — and whether acting on it pays for itself

ROC-AUC 0.846 on a held-out fifth of 7,043 telecom customers, catching 75% of churners at a threshold chosen by maximising F1 rather than left at 0.5.

The finding

Accuracy is the least interesting number: 73.5% of these customers stay, so predicting "nobody leaves" scores 73.5% and is worth nothing. What matters is the campaign — 8.2× return, and still profitable down to a 10% offer-acceptance rate, so the conclusion doesn't rest on optimism.

Smart traffic management

A low-resolution highway camera turned into telemetry

YOLOv4-tiny through cv2.dnn — no PyTorch, no CUDA — plus a centroid tracker with occlusion tolerance and a virtual counting line. 26 unique vehicles at 8.2 fps on two CPU cores.

The finding

Detection alone is not a count. Summed frame by frame the same clip gives 551 detections against 27 distinct tracks — an overstatement of roughly 20×, because a car in view for thirty frames is counted thirty times.

  • 8.2 fpson two CPU cores
  • 0.33near-field recall — stated, not hidden
